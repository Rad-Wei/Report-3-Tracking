Financial Formula Reference

Complete explanations of every formula required in Report 3, with concepts, examples, and common mistakes to avoid.

%

CAGR — Compound Annual Growth Rate

Projects how fast a market grows year over year at a constant rate

Used by Pair 1 · Section 2 market size projection
Formula
FV = PV × (1 + CAGR)^n
FV = Future Value · PV = Present Value · n = number of years
What CAGR means
CAGR represents the smooth annual rate at which something would have to grow to go from its starting value to its ending value. It ignores year-to-year volatility — it's a clean average growth rate for projection purposes.
Example walkthrough
Market today (2024): $2.1M CAD
CAGR assumed: 5% · Years: 6 (to 2030)
$2.1M × (1.05)^6 = $2.1M × 1.3401 = ~$2.81M CAD by 2030
Common mistakes
❌ Forgetting to convert to Canada-specific figures first
❌ Using the CAGR as a percentage without the formula
✅ Always state assumptions clearly if exact data is unavailable
✓ Use 4.5%–6% CAGR if segment data unavailable (state as assumption)
$

Gross Profit & Gross Margin %

Measures profitability at the product level before operating costs

Used by Pair 2 · Section 4.3 pricing strategy
Gross Profit
Gross Profit = Selling Price − Production Cost
Gross Margin %
Margin % = (Gross Profit ÷ Selling Price) × 100
Gross Profit vs. Net Profit
Gross Profit = revenue minus only the direct cost to make the product. Net Profit subtracts all operating costs (rent, salaries, marketing). Gross Margin tells you if the product pricing is viable before overhead.
Example
Sell price: $750 · Production cost: $380
Gross Profit = $750 − $380 = $370
Margin % = ($370 ÷ $750) × 100 = 49.3%
✓ Specialty assistive products benchmark: 40%–60% margin
⚠ Below 35% = raise price or reduce cost
↑

Break-Even Analysis

The point at which total revenues equal total costs — zero profit, zero loss

Used by Pair 3 · Section 5 timeline & milestones
Break-Even Units
Break-Even = Fixed Costs ÷ Gross Profit per Unit
Fixed costs vs. variable costs
Fixed costs are paid regardless of how many units are sold (patent filing, CRM, marketing setup). Variable costs change per unit (production materials). Break-even uses fixed costs only.
Example
Fixed costs = $10,000 CAD
Gross Profit per unit = $370 (from Pair 2)
Break-even = $10,000 ÷ $370 = ~27 units
Feasibility check
Ask: can Visser sell 27 Garden Carts within 12–18 months? Given community gardens + municipal targets identified in Report 1, this should be feasible — state why in the text.
⊕

CAC & CLV — Customer Economics

Cost to acquire one customer vs. total value that customer generates

Used by Pair 4 · Section 8 KPIs
Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
CAC = Total Marketing Budget ÷ New Clients Targeted
Example: $4,000 ÷ 6 = $667 CAC per client
Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)
CLV = Avg. Engagement Value × Avg. Engagements per Client
Example: $3,000 × 2 repeat engagements = $6,000 CLV
CLV:CAC Ratio (efficiency indicator)
Ratio = CLV ÷ CAC → healthy: 3:1 or higher
Example: $6,000 ÷ $667 = 9:1 — highly efficient
Why CLV:CAC matters
A ratio below 1 means you spend more acquiring a client than they generate — unsustainable. 3:1 is the minimum healthy threshold. 9:1 means the business model is efficient — every dollar of marketing returns $9 in client value.
✓ Healthy service business benchmark: CLV:CAC ≥ 3:1

Risk Score Matrix — Likelihood × Impact

Quantifies risk severity to prioritize mitigation actions — based on ISO 31000

Used by Pair 5 · Section 9 risk mitigation
Risk Score Formula
Risk Score = Likelihood (1–5) × Impact (1–5)
Maximum score = 25 · Minimum = 1 · Sum all 10 for Cumulative Score
Likelihood scale (1–5)
1 = Rare (almost impossible) · 2 = Unlikely · 3 = Possible
4 = Likely · 5 = Almost Certain
Impact scale (1–5)
1 = Negligible · 2 = Minor · 3 = Moderate
4 = Major (threatens objectives) · 5 = Catastrophic (threatens survival)
Score RangePriorityRequired Response
15–25HighImmediate mitigation action required
8–14ModerateActive monitoring + contingency plan
1–7LowPeriodic review sufficient
Example scoring
Founder dependency: Likelihood = 5 (almost certain if Marcel is sick) × Impact = 5 (catastrophic — no one else can deliver) = Score: 25 (High)
Cumulative score interpretation
Add all 10 risk scores. A total near or above 100 indicates a high-risk environment — typical of early-stage startups. Use this to frame the opening sentence of Section 9.
